Measurements on Arm hardware

Everything on this page was produced by scripts in the repository on named hardware, and the raw results are committed next to them. Rerun them, and tell us what we got wrong.

How to read this page. These are real-hardware numbers from one small virtual machine. They are not a benchmark in the sense of the project's measurement protocol (several instance types, pinned cores, repeated days, independent review), and no general performance claim is drawn from them. Their job is to find problems: the first run found twelve, all fixed and documented.

Correctness on silicon

First, it has to be right.

SVE2 kernels on real Neoverse V2

All ten hand-written SVE2 kernels pass the differential suite against scalar and NEON, the first time the assembly ran outside QEMU. Under emulation they are also checked at 256- and 512-bit vectors.

31 / 31 oracle checks

Query results on real market data match DuckDB and Polars, check for check (bench/oracle/).

Crash recovery

kill -9 during ingest of a 407,000-frame replay with WAL and segments: identical row counts after restart, serving again 1.14 s after start (20 segments re-attached in 2 ms, WAL replay 1.0 s).

1,000 power cuts

The write-ahead log under dm-flakey on ext4 in an aarch64 VM: 1,000 of 1,000 iterations without losing an acknowledged record or missing a corruption (power-loss-rig.sh).

Timing scopes differ, and that matters. “ANKAA in server” is plan, execute and collect inside ankaad. “ANKAA over Flight” is client-observed: request sent to last row received, the column to compare. DuckDB runs in process (execute and fetch Arrow, no network). ClickHouse is client-observed over HTTP, QuestDB over the Postgres protocol. Green marks the lowest median among the client-observed columns. Raw results: markdown · JSON · method.

What this does not show: concurrency, ingest under query load, larger-than-memory data, other instance sizes, or tuned configurations of the other engines. Those belong to the full benchmark, which will be reviewed before it is published.

Where ANKAA loses. Two one-host, one-hour queries, which TimescaleDB answers from an index in a few hundred microseconds while ANKAA pays 0.3 ms of Arrow Flight round trip and 0.3 ms of query planning for 30 µs of work; the newest five minutes against TimescaleDB's rowstore; and memory during a load, where ANKAA peaked at 5.1 GiB and kept it until restarted (the same data then takes 0.25 to 0.6 GiB). InfluxDB 3 Core 3.12 did not complete on this 16 GB VM (out of memory in its deduplication sort, twice) and is left out rather than tuned by us.

One VM size, four cores, one run, 1,000 series, clients on different protocols. Nothing here says how the engines compare with 100,000 series, on 64 cores or under concurrent load. The note has the method, the raw results, how the day started (ANKAA lost 11 of 15 before the changes it describes) and the profile behind every change.

Endurance

Live soaks on Google Axion.

The server runs as a hardened systemd service on live Binance streams (trades and 100 ms depth for three symbols), with WAL, segments, retention and two continuous queries. scripts/soak.sh scrapes the metrics every 30 seconds and fails on any feed gap, dropped frame, WAL error, rejected row, restart or memory growth outside the data tiers.
